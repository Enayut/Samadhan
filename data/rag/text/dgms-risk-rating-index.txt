Preface

The mining industry is characterized by diverse geological, technological and operational conditions, with
varying degrees of inherent hazards and associated risks. In this context, the adoption of a risk-based
approach at the mine level has assumed increasing importance for prioritizing resources, identifying
critical areas of concern and implementing preventive measures for reducing the likelihood and severity of
mine accidents.

With a view to strengthening this approach at the national level, it is considered desirable to develop a
robust and transparent framework for assessing the risk profile, regulatory compliance and overall
safety performance of individual mines. Such a framework would facilitate data-driven prioritization of
regulatory interventions, encourage continuous improvement in safety performance and promote a culture
of self-regulation and proactive risk management within the mining industry.

Accordingly, a Committee was constituted to examine and formulate a framework for the National Risk
Rating Index (NRRI) and the National Compliance Rating Index (NCRI). The Committee has
submitted its report containing recommendations regarding the methodology, parameters, indicators and
scoring framework for assessment of risk and compliance at the national level.

The proposed integration of the NRRI and NCRI would provide the basis for determining an Overall
Mine Safety Rating (OMSR), which is intended to provide a holistic assessment of a mine by taking into
account both its inherent risk profile and its safety management and regulatory compliance performance.
The proposed framework is envisaged as an instrument for prioritizing inspections and regulatory
resources, identifying areas requiring focused intervention, encouraging improved safety
performance and recognizing mines demonstrating exemplary safety standards.

The proposed rating framework is also intended to facilitate benchmarking of mine safety performance
against recognized good practices and, where appropriate, internationally accepted approaches, thereby
promoting continuous improvement, responsible mining and self-regulation.

Before finalization of the methodology and scoring framework for the NRRI, NCRI and OMSR, it is
considered appropriate to obtain wider views and expert inputs from all concerned stakeholders.
Accordingly, the report of the Committee is being placed in the public domain for comments and
suggestions from mine operators, workers’ representatives, researchers, academicians, professional and
technical institutions, industry experts and other stakeholders associated with mine safety and allied fields.

Stakeholders are invited to critically examine the proposed parameters, indicators, weightages, scoring
methodology and overall framework and furnish specific, evidence-based and constructive
comments/suggestions within fifteen (15) days from the date of publication of this document. The inputs
received will be examined appropriately and may be considered while finalizing the National Risk Rating
Index, National Compliance Rating Index and Overall Mine Safety Rating framework.

The objective of this consultation is to develop a credible, transparent, objective, practicable and
continuously improvable national mine safety rating system that strengthens preventive safety
management, facilitates effective regulatory intervention and promotes a sustained culture of risk
management, compliance and self-regulation across the Indian mining industry.

                                     BACKGROUND

1.0 Background Information:

1.1 Mining, by its very nature, is a high-risk economic activity involving complex
    interactions between geological, technical, and human factors. In India, where
    mining contributes significantly to the national economy and supports critical sectors
    such as energy, infrastructure, and manufacturing, the scale of operations is vast
    ranging from deep underground coal mines and large opencast metal mines to
    extensive oil and gas fields. Given its inherent hazards and socio-economic
    importance, the need for a robust, scientific, and proactive safety management
    framework is paramount. The Indian mining-safety regulatory framework is
    governed by the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020,
    along with the rules, regulations, and orders made thereunder, and is administered
    by the Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS). Over time, the regulatory
    framework has gradually evolved from a prescriptive, rule-based approach towards
    a risk-based and performance-oriented model of safety governance. In this evolving
    regulatory environment, two complementary indices the National Risk Rating Index
    (NRRI) and the National Compliance Rating Index (NCRI) have been conceptualized
    to modernize the assessment of mine safety and promote sustainable and
    responsible mining practices.
1.2 Building upon these frameworks, the integration of the National Risk Rating Index
    (NRRI) and the National Compliance Rating Index (NCRI) leads to the development
    of an Overall Mine Safety Rating (OMSR), which holistically represents both the
    inherent risk profile and the safety management performance of a mine. This
    composite rating enables a balanced evaluation - recognizing that even a high-risk
    mine can operate safely if robust compliance systems, monitoring technologies, and
    proactive safety cultures are in place. Conversely, low-risk mines with poor
    compliance can still pose significant hazards. By combining these two indices
    through a weighted or normalized model, the OMSR provides a scientific and
    transparent basis for classifying mines, prioritizing inspections, allocating regulatory
    resources, and incentivizing good safety practices. In the Indian regulatory context,
    such an integrated rating system supports DGMS in data-driven decision-making,
    continuous safety improvement, and sustainable resource development aligned with
    national safety and ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) goals.

1.3 Earlier National Safety Award (NSA) was conferred to mines annually based on LAFP
    (Longest Accident-Free Period), LIFR (Lowest Injury Frequency Rate), LIFRM
    (Lowest Injury Frequency Rate on Man shift), LIFRO (Lowest Injury Frequency Rate
    based on output (composite of ore/coal and overburden). The criteria adopted was
    not comprehensive.

                           NATIONAL RISK RATING SYSTEM
      The following major risk factors are considered for Coal, Metalliferous and Oil Mines
      separately. Further, in coal & metalliferous mines risk factor has been separately
      considered for belowground and opencast mines.
      Risk Rating system has been proposed in three formats, covering (i) belowground
      mines (both coal and metalliferous), (ii) Open cast (both coal and metalliferous),
      and (iii) Oil mines. Risk rating system assigns risk score for broad categories based
      on risk score generated on the basis of risk values assign for risk sub-categories.
      The assignment of risk score shall be system based, on the basis of the data
      submitted by mine management.
      Total score on adding the individual category score gives the inherent Risk score of
      a mine. Risk score arrived for each risk category is residual risk score which is
      difference of inherent risk score and risk reduction score. Risk reduction score is
      calculated on the basis of mitigatory measures taken by mine management against
      that particular risk category or sub-category.
      Accordingly, format for submission of details by individual mine has been designed
      to consider all risk categories, mitigatory measures and a manner to arrive at value
      of residual risk. This format is designed separately for (i) belowground mines (both
      coal and metalliferous), (ii) Open cast (both coal and metalliferous), and (iii) Oil
      mines.

                          NATIONAL COMPLIANCE RATING INDEX

6.1   The National Compliance Rating Index (NCRI) is an assessment system designed to
      evaluate how well mines comply with safety laws, standards, and best practices as
      prescribed in the legislation and regulatory bodies.

      Unlike the Risk Rating Index, which measures how risky a mine’s environment is,
      the Compliance Index measures how effectively a mine’s management and
      workforce follow safety systems, rules, and preventive measures. It provides a
      quantitative score for each mine, reflecting its overall safety culture, management
      effectiveness, and regulatory compliance.

      The NCRI framework will evaluates following major dimensions of mine safety
      compliance, each consisting of several measurable parameters:

6.1.1 Regulatory Compliance
      Adherence to the OSH&WC Code, 2020 (erstwhile the Mines Act, 1952)

          •   The Coal Mines Regulations, 2017/ Metalliferous Mines Regulations, 1961 /
              Oil Mines Regulations, 2017 (250)
          •   The OSH&WC (Central) Rules, 2026 (200)
          •   The Central Electricity Authority (Measures relating to Safety and Electric
              Supply) Regulations, 2023 (50)
          •   Timely submission of statutory reports & returns (25)
          •   Notice issued under Sec. 38(1)B(a) and Sec. 38(1)B(k) of the OSH&WC Code,
              2020 (50)
          •   Order issued under Sec. 38(1)B(b), 38(1)B(c), 38(1)B(d) and Sec. 38(1)B(l)
              of the OSH&WC Code, 2020 and in force for more than a year (50)
          •   Long pending Violations (more than six months) (50)

7.1   Overall Mine Safety Rating (OMSR) should combine both NRRI and NCRI,
      showing how effectively a mine with a certain inherent risk level is managing its
      safety.
      Conceptual Relationship

      The relationship can be visualized as:

                        "Safety Performance"=f ("Risk Level”, “Compliance Level")

      A high-risk mine with high compliance can still be safe.

      A low-risk mine with poor compliance can become unsafe.

      Hence, both indices must be balanced to represent true safety status.

7.2   Proposed Mathematical Model

      NRRI: Normalized Risk Rating Index (0 to 100)

      NCRI: Normalized Compliance Rating Index (0 to 100)

      OMSR: Overall Mine Safety Rating (0 to 100)

      Since higher NRRI means higher risk and higher NCRI means better safety,

      we need a formula where risk acts inversely to compliance.


      Option 1: Weighted Inverse Relationship


              𝑶𝑴𝑺𝑹 = (𝑾𝟏 × 𝑵𝑪𝑹𝑰) + [𝑾𝟐 × (𝟏𝟎𝟎 − 𝑵𝑹𝑹𝑰)]


                      Where:

                      𝑊1 = Weight for compliance (e.g., 0.6)

                      𝑊2 = Weight for risk mitigation (e.g., 0.4)

      Option 2: Normalized Index (Balanced View)
                            𝑵𝑪𝑹𝑰
               𝑶𝑴𝑺𝑹 =              ×𝑪
                          (𝑵𝑹𝑹𝑰+𝟏)


                      where 𝐶 is a scaling constant (≈100) to fit the output range 0 - 100.
                      This is simple, effective, and easy for annual benchmarking.

      After deliberations it was decided that Option 2, which gives Normalized Index is
      most suitable for Calculation of Overall Mine Safety Rating.

7.4   Advantages of the Combined Rating (Overall Mine Safety Rating):

      Integrates both inherent hazards and human/system performance; Enables
      objective comparison across different mines types; Supports prioritized inspections
      and resource allocation; Encourages continuous improvement and competition for
      safety excellence; Provides a scientific tool for policy, ESG reporting, and investment
      decisions.

      The establishment of unified indices such as the NRRI and NCRI, and their
      integration into an Overall Mine Safety Rating (OMSR), marks a decisive step toward
      a modern, data-centric safety governance system in India’s mining industry.

      This framework not only enables objective comparison and benchmarking across
      diverse mine types—coal, metal, and oil—but also aligns regulatory oversight with
      the principles of risk-based inspection and preventive safety management.

      By leveraging these indices, DGMS and mining organizations can jointly pursue
      continuous risk reduction, technology-driven monitoring, and capacity building at
      all operational levels.

                                     SUBMISSION PROCESS


8.1   Self-Submission forms:
      Based on the framework, self-submission forms will be developed for different
      category of mines which will be filled by the mine management themselves digitally.
      The form will capture data objectively as far as practical to make the evaluation
      system more transparent and objective.

      An Online portal shall be developed to facilitate submission of forms by the mine
      management. Mines which fail to digitally self-submit the prescribed forms within
      the stipulated time may either be assigned the lowest rating category, such as “E”
      rating, or an alternate mechanism may be developed for obtaining and entering the
      requisite information through empanelled third-party auditors or other authorized
      agencies at the cost of the mine management.
8.2   Verification System:

      Online verification: The data submitted by the mine management in the online portal shall be
      verified with existing records and information available

      Physical verification: The entries of the high overall safety rated mines shall be verified through
      physical inspections. Similarly, entries of the mine with abnormally low safety score may also be
      verified physically.

      To ensure reliability and integrity of the submitted data, provision may also be made
      for periodic verification, random audits, cross-validation with inspection findings,
      and third-party assessment wherever considered necessary

      This submission of the prescribed forms for the National Risk Rating Index (NRRI)
      and National Compliance Rating Index (NCRI) will be mandatory for all mines across
      the country, irrespective of category, mineral, size, or ownership.

      Repeated non-submission or deliberate suppression of information may also be
      considered as an adverse compliance factor while determining the overall mine
      safety rating.
8.3   Software Development:
      A dedicated and integrated software system may be developed for implementation
      of the National Risk Rating Index (NRRI) and National Compliance Rating Index
      (NCRI) framework. The software shall function as a centralized digital platform for
      submission, processing, validation, analysis, and monitoring of mine-related safety
      and compliance data across the country.

reliability, and secure handling of large volumes of data generated from mines of
different categories and minerals. The system may include separate user interfaces
for mine managements, DGMS officials, third-party auditors, and administrators
with role-based access controls and authentication mechanisms.

The platform may provide online self-submission modules with predefined formats,
automated data validation checks, field verification, logical consistency checks, and
document upload facilities to minimize errors and improve quality of submitted data.
Provision may also be incorporated for digital signatures, electronic
acknowledgements, and audit trails to ensure authenticity and traceability of
submissions.

The system should automatically generate National Risk Rating Index (NRRI),
National Compliance Rating Index (NCRI), and Overall Mine Safety Ratings based
on predefined algorithms.

Provision may further be made for integration of the software with existing
government digital platforms such as the Shram Suvidha Portal to generate risk
based inspection.

The software may also incorporate Artificial Intelligence (AI) and data analytics tools
for identification of abnormal trends, predictive risk assessment, early warning
generation, and prioritization of mines for inspections under the risk-based
randomized inspection system.

At a later stage this system can be further upgraded for automated alerts for
delayed submissions, adverse safety indicators, recurring violations, increasing
accident trends, or sudden deterioration in compliance levels.

                           OUTCOME & RECOMMENDATIONS

9.1 Outcomes:
      (a). The proposed framework is expected to promote a culture continuous
           improvement and responsible mining practices.

      (b). The Overall Mine Safety Rating derived from the aforesaid indices is expected to
           promote accountability and healthy competition among mine operators towards
           achieving higher standards of safety and compliance.

      (c). The system may also incentivize timely reporting of accidents, dangerous
           occurrences, and near-miss incidents, thereby strengthening preventive safety
           culture in the mining sector.
9.2    Recommendations:
      (a). It is recommended that submission of the prescribed forms for the National Risk
           Rating Index (NRRI) and National Compliance Rating Index (NCRI) be made
           mandatory for all mines across the country, irrespective of category, mineral,
           size, or ownership.

      (b). The Overall Mine Safety Rating shall be publicized in web portal for information.
           This will serve as motivation for higher overall mine safety rating.

      (c). The overall mine safety rating shall be incorporated in Shram Suvidha portal and
           shall be factored into the risk-based randomized inspection selection process.
           Mines with Higher OMSR may be subjected to reduced inspection frequency.
           Mines with poor ratings may receive enhanced and targeted inspections.

      (d). Periodic review and updating of the indices may be undertaken to ensure
           alignment with current trends.

      (e). It is also recommended that a structured reward and recognition system be
           instituted for mines with higher Overall Safety Rating Index. As system of
           incentivising such mines by other government departments may also be thought
           upon. Such mines may be facilitated for grant of future leases and other
           clearances.

      (f). A detailed notification in this regard may be issued so as to formalise the whole
           process and give a legitimate dimension to it.
